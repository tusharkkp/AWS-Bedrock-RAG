1. PROJECT OVERVIEW
Project Nova is an autonomous deep-space probe initiative developed by Nova Spaceworks.
Its primary objective is the exploration of subterranean ice fissures on Jupiter's moon, Europa.
The mission launch date is formally scheduled for October 14, 2027, from Launch Complex 39A.

2. POWER SYSTEMS & PROPULSION
- Power Plant: The probe utilizes a dual NextGen Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (RTG-X2).
- Operating Lifetime: The RTG-X2 is rated for 18 years of continuous power output at 450 Watts.
- Propulsion: Primary transit propulsion uses the Xenon-Hall Ion Drive "Aeon-4", capable of
  delivering continuous low-thrust efficiency with a specific impulse of 4,200 seconds.
- Secondary Maneuvering: Cold-gas nitrogen thrusters are used for delicate proximity maneuvers.

3. SCIENTIFIC INSTRUMENT SUITE
- DeepCryo Radar (DCR): Sub-surface penetration radar operating at 15 MHz and 50 MHz frequencies,
  capable of imaging liquid water layers up to 4 kilometers beneath solid surface ice.
- SpectroChroma Mass Spectrometer: Real-time molecular analysis of plume vapors with sensitivity
  up to 1 part per billion.
- MicroLIDAR Array: Real-time 3D topographical reconstruction for autonomous obstacle avoidance.

4. AUTONOMOUS FLIGHT COMPUTER
- Core Processor: Radiation-hardened Dual Quad-Core RAD750 running at 200 MHz.
- Flight Software: NovaOS v4.2, built on microkernel architecture with automated failover.
- Data Storage: 4 Terabytes of error-correcting solid-state memory.

6. EMERGENCY SAFE MODES
In the event of attitude loss exceeding 15 degrees from Earth point, the probe automatically
enters "Safe Mode Sun-Lock" within 120 seconds, spinning up reaction wheels to stabilize and
broadcasting a low-gain beacon signal at 2.2 GHz.
